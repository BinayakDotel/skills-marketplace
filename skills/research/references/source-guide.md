# Where to look, by topic type

Read only the section for your topic. The ranking in `SKILL.md` (primary → academic → institutional → news → encyclopedia) always applies.

## Myths, religion, festivals, rituals

- **Find the text first.** Most stories trace to a named text: a Purana, an epic, a chronicle (vamsavali), a local mahatmya. Search `<story> <text name> translation` and open a full translation (wisdomlib.org, sacred-texts.com, GRETIL for Sanskrit). Quote the translation and name the translator in `publisher` when shown.
- **Expect several versions.** Regions, communities and later retellings differ. Make one `versions` entry each; never pick one silently.
- **Separate text from practice.** "The text says X" (`legend`) and "people today do Y" (`practice`) are different facts with different sources.
- **Later traditions are not the original text.** If a popular detail (a number of days, a second hero, a date) is not in the text, record it as a later tradition with its own source, or as a `dont_say` entry if it is presented as scripture.
- **Dates of festivals** come from the national calendar authority (in Nepal, the Nepal Panchang Nirnayak Bikas Samiti) or news that quotes it. They change every year; mark them `current` with the year.

## History, heritage sites, kings and dynasties

- Prefer inscriptions, archaeological reports, official heritage pages (UNESCO, the national department of archaeology, the site's managing body) and academic histories.
- "Oldest", "first", "only" and exact founding years are the most copied and least checked claims. Demand a strong source or move them to `disputed` / `dont_say`.
- Note what changed and when: built, damaged, rebuilt, renamed. Each is its own fact.

## Science, health, nature

- Peer-reviewed papers, government and international agencies, university pages, established science publications.
- Folk explanations go in as `legend` or `practice`, never as `science`.

## Numbers and statistics

- Find the original publisher of the number (census office, ministry, survey, paper). Record the year the number applies to, not the year an article repeated it.
- Round only in `claim_local` or the script, never in the quote.

## Recent events and news

- At least two established outlets for anything important; prefer the most recent update.
- Put "as of <date>" in every `current` claim. Early reports change: say so in `notes`.
- Living people: report what they did and said with attribution. No speculation about motives.

## Red flags

- No author, no date, no sources of its own.
- The same sentence on many sites (copied text counts once).
- Tour, hotel and astrology sites stating history as fact.
- AI-written listicles ("10 amazing facts about…").
- Quotes attributed to famous people with no original source.
- "Scientists say" or "studies show" without naming who.

## Search tips

- Search in the topic's own language and script as well as English: `पशुपतिनाथ उत्पत्ति`, `दशैं इतिहास`.
- Add `translation`, `inscription`, `excavation`, `pdf`, or `site:edu`, `site:gov.np`, `site:unesco.org` to reach better sources.
- Open Wikipedia's References section and go to the best one or two sources it cites.
